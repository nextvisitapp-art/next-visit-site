## The product

Next Visit is a planning and memory app for couples. Planning and calendar,
trips and visits, shared lists and notes, memories and photos, Song of the Day,
and a relationship profile with anniversary and birthdays. It has a
long-distance mode and a same-city mode; the mode changes assumptions and copy
only, never features.

Live on the App Store at `id6775123226`. Two AU ratings, zero US. Also a web
planner at `go.next-visit.app` and a marketing site at `hello.next-visit.app`.

**A web-first pivot is in progress.** Android is 40.5% of mobile in Australia,
and the app needs both partners, so under an iOS-only model roughly a third of
couples could both use it. The plan is to promote the web planner and let iOS
users install afterwards. Until that lands, the CTA is still the App Store.
Check with Charlie before changing the CTA in a video.

## What we sell

Sell the trip, not the app. The feature is never the subject. The subject is a
thing that happens between two people, and the app is how it happened.

The counter-example is the whole first four posts: correct information,
good typography, and nobody watched. Charlie's own verdict, and it is the most
useful sentence in this document: *"even I don't wanna watch them as a
consumer."*

## Non-negotiables

These are Charlie's, stated by him, and they do not get traded off.

1. **Faceless.** Screen recordings and hands. He is never on camera.
2. **No em dashes anywhere.** Hyphens only. This applies to on-screen text,
   captions, and anything written for him to read.
3. **Judge on watch-through and saves. Never views.** Reel one got 1,029 views
   and 0 saves.
4. **No burned-in watermarks and no burned-in music.** Both break TikTok API
   posting and forfeit the audio-page ranking signal. Charlie adds a platform
   library track at post time.
5. **His own voice, never AI.** Decided 3 August. The audience is 86% female,
   18 to 34, and that cohort reads synthetic narration as dropshipping. A real
   voice is the only asset nobody can copy on an account with no face.
6. **Footage briefs are 2 to 3 minute continuous takes, not short clips.**
   Long footage is what the clipping tools need.
7. **Below roughly 20 to 30 posts, say the sample is too small** rather than
   inventing a pattern from it.
8. **Keep messages to Charlie very short.** A shot list, a decision he needs to
   make, or a number that changed something. No strategy essays.

## What the data actually says

Four posts as at 3 August, roughly 2,400 views.

| Post | Date | Length | Views | Avg watch | Watched | Skip rate | Saves |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| dayzero-nofaces | 27 Jul | 14s | 1,086 | 5s | 36% | 72.6% | 0 |
| planner-stopsaying | 29 Jul | 9s | 295 | 4s | 44% | 84.1% | 0 |
| Carousel 01 | 30 Jul | stills | 215 | n/a | n/a | n/a | 0 |
| Video 03, the surprise | 2 Aug | 16.3s | 474 | 4s | 24% | 69.2% | 0 |

**Finding one. Skip rate is improving and the cause is known.** 84.1 to 72.6 to
69.2. The one variable that changed is the opening frame. The worst opened on a
UI screen; the best two open on motion with the headline already on screen at
frame zero. Skip rate to beat is **69.2%**.

**Finding two. Absolute watch time is a constant. Length is not.** Average watch
is 4 to 5 seconds regardless of whether the video runs 9 seconds or 16. TikTok
states plainly that most viewers stopped at 0:02. Length only changes the
fraction people see, and that went 44% to 36% to 24% as the videos got longer.
This was the hub's error, repeated across three videos before the data caught
it.

**Therefore: videos are 10 to 12 seconds.** Not 16. Video 04 was rebuilt from
17.1s down to 11.9s for this reason.

Saves are the goal but have produced zero signal across four posts, and
Instagram now labels the save rate *Typical* rather than *Lower*, meaning zero
is normal at this size. Until a post records a save, judge creative on **skip
rate first, watched fraction second**.

## Format spec

Match videos 03 to 06. The pattern that produced them:

- **1080x1920, 24fps, h264.** Rendered as HTML and CSS in headless Chromium at
  `device_scale_factor=1`, frames out to PNG, encoded with ffmpeg. The hub's
  scripts `reel4.py` through `reel12.py` are the reference implementation and
  Charlie can have them handed over.
- **10 to 12 seconds. Three to five beats.**
- **Frame zero already has the hook line on screen, over motion.** Never a UI
  screen, never a blank beat, never a fade up from black.
- **One product truth per video.** Repetition across posts is a bigger risk
  than volume.
- **The app is always a live screen recording playing**, held in a phone panel
  on navy with a masked bottom edge, roughly 720 to 760px wide. Never cropped
  stills. Stills read as an ad; a recording of someone using the thing reads as
  a person showing you something. Charlie asked for this explicitly.
- **The last shot carries both the instruction and the end card, with no cut
  between them.**
- **Spoken hook, ten to fifteen words, landing inside the first three seconds.**
  Charlie's voice, recorded as a voice memo. Not narration across the whole
  video. The two-second drop-off is the problem being solved. First test was
  video 05.

## Visual system

Colours: navy-800 `#0e2240`, navy-950 `#05111f`, cream-100 `#fbe8de`,
cream-300 `#eccab4`, pink-500 `#ec4079`, tile background `#06122a`.

Type: **Fraunces 600** for headings, with the accent word in pink italic.
**DM Mono 500** for uppercase wide-tracked eyebrows. **Space Grotesk 700** for
brand mark glyphs only. **Inter** for body. Loaded as base64 woff2 from
`@fontsource` so the render is deterministic.

Brand marks: `FlapChar` split-flap tiles. `LogoA` is nine tiles spelling
NEXTVISIT. `AppIcon` is two borderless FlapChars, N in cream and V in pink,
corner radius `size * 0.2237`.

Animation: opacity ramps use smoothstep, not ease-out cubic. Ease-out cubic
jumps 37% in the first frame and reads as a pop. Translate uses
`ease(x) = 1 - (1-x)^3`.

Two things that will bite:

- **`-noautorotate` on every ffmpeg extraction.** The transcode baked rotation
  into the pixels and left a spurious `rotation=-90` container flag. Without
  the flag every camera clip comes out sideways. The ten screen recordings
  carry no flag, so it is a safe no-op there.
- **Clamp beats to clip length.** Several clips run under 2 seconds. A beat
  overrunning its clip produced black frames in reel 02. Hold the last frame
  instead. Video 05 v1 overran and held on the iOS Control Centre.

consecutive frames at 216x384. Median 5 to 8 is normal. Anything above about 30
is a glitch. That is how the white flash was caught, at 59 against a normal 7.

## Higgsfield

Charlie is on Ultra. Rules:

- **Generated b-roll is for shots that cannot be got, not for convenience.** The
  departure board is the legitimate case: the only real one is unusable because
  of the political ad. Abstract and atmospheric only. Never anything that
  claims to be Charlie's actual life.
- **Never generated voice.** See non-negotiable 5.
- **Run the Virality Predictor before posting**, and calibrate it first against
  `reel-planner-stopsaying` and the other rendered reels already on the branch.
  If it does not score the known failures low, do not trust it.
- **Importing by URL:** `raw.githubusercontent.com` fails because it serves
  `application/octet-stream`. Use jsDelivr, which sets `video/mp4`:
  `https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/nextvisitapp-art/next-visit-site@footage/clips/<file>.mp4`
- One generated clip exists and **has had no QA**: an 8s split-flap departure
  board, 9:16, Veo 3.1, generated 4 August. The hub cannot download Higgsfield
  output so nobody has watched it. Do not cut it in until Charlie has.

## Captions

Written by the hub, not by production. Pattern that has been used: the story in
two or three plain sentences, then a question to the reader, then "Free on the
App Store", then three to five hashtags. No emoji. No hype. The caption is
where the app gets named, so the video does not have to.
